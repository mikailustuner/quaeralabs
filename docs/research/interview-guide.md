# Researcher interview guide (Phase 0)

**Target:** 10 interviews (at least 4 mathematicians, at least 4 ML researchers). Duration: 30–40 minutes.
**Purpose:** Learn real use cases and build the candidate list for the closed alpha (Phase 4). Listen, don't sell the product.

## Before the interview
- Ask the participant whether the interview may be recorded; notes are kept anonymous.
- Don't explain the product up front; spend the first 20 minutes only on how they work today.

## Questions

**Current way of working**
1. Could you walk me through a research project from the last three months, start to finish? Which step took the most time?
2. How do you check whether an idea has been tried before? How often do you skip this?
3. When did you last try to reproduce someone else's result? What happened?
4. What happens to your negative results?
5. (Math) Do you use Lean or another proof assistant? Why / why not?
6. (ML) How do you decide on the cost of an experiment (GPU, API)? Who approves it?

**Use of AI**
7. Where do you currently use AI in your research, and where do you definitely not use it?
8. What would you need to see to trust a result produced by an AI?
9. Which decisions would you never leave to an AI team?

**Concept test** (last 10 minutes; short explanation + lab screen design)
10. In which of your work would this tool be useful? In which would it not?
11. Approval cards and budget cap: too much, too little, about right?
12. How much would you consider reasonable to pay for one research run (with your own API key)?
13. Would you like to join the closed alpha? Which real question would you bring?

## After the interview
- Within 24 hours, enter the notes into `alpha-candidates.csv` and a summary file.
- Extract at most three "surprising findings" from each interview; they test the assumptions in the roadmap.
